Glymphatic System: How Sleep Cleans Your Brain

Updated June 2026. Sources: Brain Behav 2026 review

Sleep isn't just rest. During deep slow-wave sleep, your brain activates the glymphatic system, a waste clearance network that removes beta-amyloid and tau up to 60% more efficiently than wakefulness. Failure of this system may explain why chronic sleep loss doubles dementia risk.

Discovery

Maiken Nedergaard's team discovered CSF enters along periarterial spaces, exchanges with ISF, then exits via perivenous routes. Astrocytes gate flow with aquaporin-4 (AQP4). AQP4 knockout mice show ~55% reduction in amyloid clearance.

Why Sleep Flips the Switch

1) Interstitial space expands 60% (14% to 23%). 2) Norepinephrine from locus coeruleus drops, releasing vasoconstriction. 3) Infraslow oscillations drive a neurovascular pump. Zolpidem suppresses these oscillations — sleep occurs but clearance drops.

Human Evidence

The 2026 review synthesizes human DTI-ALPS MRI data showing lower clearance in insomnia and sleep apnea, correlating with plasma amyloid accumulation.

Interventions

Melatonin: 2024 study restored AQP4 polarization in sleep-restricted mice. PERK inhibition: 2024 study restored localization and reduced pathology.

Practical Takeaways

Protect slow-wave sleep, keep consistent timing, treat apnea, exercise 150 min/week, avoid chronic debt.
